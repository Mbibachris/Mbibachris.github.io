About

I'm an MPhil Economics candidate at the University of Cape Coast (UCC), Ghana, working as a Research Assistant in the School of Economics. I combine econometrics, Bayesian statistics, and machine learning to answer applied questions in development economics: from undernutrition and birth spacing, to public debt sustainability, to multidimensional poverty. My current thesis examines Chinese official development finance and household deprivation in Sub-Saharan Africa, using the AidData GCDF v3.0 and Afrobarometer Round 9 datasets.

My broader research interests sit at the intersection of applied microeconomics, food systems, and computational methods. I'm drawn to problems where household survey data, machine learning, and Bayesian networks can sharpen how we identify vulnerable households, forecast fiscal risk, and map poverty at a finer, more actionable level than traditional approaches allow. I place a lot of importance on technology and data-driven policy: building reproducible pipelines in R and Python, working with open datasets like LSMS-ISA, AidData, and Afrobarometer, and making sure the evidence behind a policy recommendation is as rigorous and reproducible as the recommendation itself.
