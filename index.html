About

I'm an MPhil Economics candidate at the University of Cape Coast (UCC), Ghana, and a Research Assistant in the School of Economics. I graduated with First Class Honours and three Dean's Awards. I have one single-authored paper submitted to the Journal of African Economics and three co-authored manuscripts under review, drawing on GLSS, AHIES, DHS, Afrobarometer and AidData microdata. My thesis asks whether household deprivation in Sub-Saharan Africa falls in places that receive Chinese official development finance.

My research interests sit at the intersection of applied microeconomics, development finance and computational methods. I'm drawn to problems where household survey data, causal inference, Bayesian modelling and machine learning can sharpen how we identify vulnerable households, target support and evaluate policy. I place a lot of importance on technology and data-driven policy: building reproducible pipelines, shipping models as tested and containerised services, and making sure the evidence behind a recommendation is as rigorous and reproducible as the recommendation itself.

Under Review

  • Not the Crisis but the Recovery: State Dependence in Vulnerable Employment in Ghana

    Mbiba, C. (2026). Single-authored. Submitted to the Journal of African Economics, September 2026. Available on SSRN.

    Dynamic random-effects probit models with the Wooldridge initial-conditions correction on three within-year quarterly panels from Ghana's AHIES 2022–2024. Net of worker heterogeneity, last quarter's vulnerable employment raises this quarter's probability of vulnerable employment by 9.5 percentage points in 2022, 16.3 in 2023 and 3.3 in 2024, peaking in the adjustment year rather than the crisis year.

Poverty Targeting System

Python · scikit-learn · LightGBM · SHAP · FastAPI · Docker · Azure Container Apps · GitHub Actions · Streamlit

An end-to-end proxy-means test that identifies multidimensionally poor households from 22 short questionnaire items, using Ghana DHS 2022 microdata. It reaches a test ROC-AUC of 0.89 and captures 72% of poor people at a 25% targeting budget (versus 25% under random selection). The measurement base was validated by reproducing the global MPI from raw survey files, and the model ships as a containerised FastAPI service with per-household SHAP explanations and a CI/CD pipeline running 180+ tests.

devecon: Python Package for Poverty Measurement

Python · NumPy · pandas · pytest · GitHub Actions

An open-source, survey-weight-aware toolkit for Foster-Greer-Thorbecke and Alkire-Foster poverty measures, with modular identification and aggregation functions. Continuous integration keeps it reliable across Python 3.9 to 3.12, and it now serves as the measurement engine of the Poverty Targeting System.

Reproducible Research Data Pipeline

R · fuzzy record linkage

For my MPhil thesis, a five-script R pipeline links about 52,000 Afrobarometer Round 9 respondents in 38 countries to AidData GCDF v3.0 project records. It reconciles place names across administrative boundaries with Jaro-Winkler fuzzy matching and documents quality checks at each stage.

Degrees Without Dividends: Returns to Education in Ghana

Data for Impact Datathon, University of Cape Coast · GLSS 7

Quantifies how returns to schooling differ between Ghana's formal and informal sectors, using Mincerian earnings regressions, sheepskin effects and an Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition.
